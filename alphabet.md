# The Old English Alphabet & Pronunciation

Old English uses a modified Latin alphabet. It has no *j*, *k*, *q*, *v*,
or *z* in native spelling, plus a handful of letters not used in Modern
English. Vowel length is phonemic — a long vowel changes a word's meaning,
not just its stress — and is marked with a macron (ā) in modern scholarly
editions, though the original manuscripts rarely mark it. Source: general
Old English phonology as described in Mitchell & Robinson, *A Guide to Old
English*; examples drawn from words already in this repository.

## Special Letters

| Letter | Name | Sound | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| æ | æsc ("ash") | like the *a* in Modern English "cat" | Also used as a vowel-length pair: ǣ is the long version |
| þ | þorn ("thorn") | "th", either voiced (as in "this") or voiceless (as in "thin") | Interchangeable with ð by scribes — no meaningful distinction was made between them |
| ð | eð ("eth") | "th", either voiced or voiceless, same as þ | Often alternates with þ within the very same word across manuscripts |
| ƿ | wynn | like Modern English *w* | Modern printed editions (including this repository) normally replace it with *w* for readability, since it's easily confused with *p* |
| ċ, ġ | c/g with a dot | signal the "soft" pronunciation (see Consonant Rules below) | A modern editorial convention, not found in the original manuscripts |

## Vowels

| Letter(s) | Length | Approximate sound | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| a | short | like *a* in "father", shortened | *land* "land" |
| ā | long | like *a* in "father", held longer | *bān* "bone" |
| æ | short | like *a* in "cat" | *þæt* "that" |
| ǣ | long | like *a* in "cat", held longer | *dǣd* "deed" |
| e | short | like *e* in "bed" | *helm* "helmet" |
| ē | long | like *ay* in "day" (no glide) | *hē* "he" |
| i | short | like *i* in "bit" | *him* "him/it" |
| ī | long | like *ee* in "feet" | *wīn* "wine" |
| o | short | like *o* in "hot" (British) | *god* "god" |
| ō | long | like *o* in "note" (no glide) | *gōd* "good" |
| u | short | like *u* in "put" | *sunu* "son" |
| ū | long | like *oo* in "food" | *hūs* "house" |
| y | short | like German ü / French u — round your lips while saying "i" | *synn* "sin" |
| ȳ | long | the same rounded vowel, held longer | *fȳr* "fire" |
| ea, eo, ie | diphthong | a glide between the two vowel qualities, roughly one syllable | *heofon* "heaven", *eorl* "nobleman" |

## Consonant Rules

| Letter(s) | Rule | Example | Gloss |
|---|---|---|---|
| c | "hard" /k/ before a back vowel (a, o, u) or a consonant | *cyning* → /ˈkyning/ | "king" |
| c | "soft" /tʃ/ ("ch") before or after a front vowel (i, e) or after i | *ic* → /itʃ/ | "I" |
| g | "hard" /g/ before a back vowel | *gār* → /gɑːr/ | "spear" |
| g | "soft" /j/ (like *y* in "yes") before or after a front vowel | *geong* → /jeong/ | "young" |
| g | between back vowels, a voiced fricative (like Dutch *g*) | *dagas* | "days" |
| sc | /ʃ/ ("sh") | *scip* → /ʃip/ | "ship" |
| cg | /dʒ/ ("j" as in "judge") | *brycg* → /brydʒ/ | "bridge" |
| h | /h/ at the start of a word | *hē* | "he" |
| h | further back in the throat (like German *ch* in "Bach") after a back vowel, medially or finally | *niht* | "night" |
| f | voiced, like *v*, between vowels | *ofer* → /over/ | "over" |
| f | voiceless, like *f*, elsewhere | *feond* | "enemy" |
| s | voiced, like *z*, between vowels | *ceosan* → /keozan/ | "to choose" |
| þ / ð | voiced, like *th* in "this", between vowels | *bróðor* | "brother" |
| þ / ð | voiceless, like *th* in "thin", elsewhere | *þēof* | "thief" |
| r | trilled or tapped, not like the Modern English *r* | *rōd* | "cross" |
| double consonants | held noticeably longer than a single consonant | *sunne* vs. *sunu* | "sun" vs. "son" |
