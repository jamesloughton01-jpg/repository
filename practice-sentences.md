# Practice Sentences (Original Compositions)

**Not from any historical text** — these are original sentences composed
for this repository, built from the vocabulary in `vocabulary.md` and the
grammar rules in `grammar.md`, to demonstrate specific case/verb patterns
in isolation. Treat them as constructed practice material, not attested
Old English usage — for real usage, see `texts/` and `sentences.md`.
Grammar reference: Mitchell & Robinson, *A Guide to Old English*.

## Nominative & simple statements (wesan "to be")

| Old English | Modern English | Grammar note |
|---|---|---|
| Se cyning is gōd. | The king is good. | *se* = nom. masc. sg. "the"; *is* = 3sg pres. of *wesan* |
| Sēo cwēn is wīs. | The queen is wise. | *sēo* = nom. fem. sg. "the" |
| Þæt cild is glæd. | The child is glad. | *þæt* = nom./acc. neut. sg. "the" |
| Wæs se cyning gōd? | Was the king good? | yes/no questions front the verb |
| Hwæt is þīn nama? | What is your name? | *þīn* agrees with *nama* (masc.) |
| Se munuc is hālig. | The monk is holy. | *munuc* is masc.; predicate adjectives here stay uninflected, as elsewhere in this list |
| Þā cnihtas sind glæde. | The boys are glad. | *sind* = pl. pres. of *wesan*; *glæde* = strong pl. adjective ending |
| Ic eom sēoc. | I am sick. | 1sg. pres. of *wesan* = *eom* |
| Þū eart strang. | You are strong. | 2sg. pres. of *wesan* = *eart* |
| Wæs þæt scip hēah? | Was that ship tall? | yes/no question fronting *wæs*, as with *Wæs se cyning gōd?* |

## Accusative (direct objects, habban "to have")

| Old English | Modern English | Grammar note |
|---|---|---|
| Ic hæbbe sweord. | I have a sword. | *sweord* is neut., same form nom./acc. |
| Þū hæfst bōc. | You have a book. | *bōc* is a root-consonant noun; acc. sg. = *bōc* |
| Hē hæfð hors. | He has a horse. | *habban* is irregular: *hæbbe, hæfst, hæfð* |
| Wē habbaþ frēondas. | We have friends. | plural present is *habbaþ* for all persons |
| Ne hæbbe ic sweord. | I do not have a sword. | negation fronts *ne*, forcing verb-subject inversion |
| Hēo hæfð rōd. | She has a cross. | *rōd* is fem.; regular acc. sg. = *rōd*, same as nom. |
| Ic hæbbe bēc. | I have books. | *bēc* = acc. pl. of *bōc* (i-mutation, cf. sg. *bōc*) |
| Þā cnihtas habbaþ gāras. | The boys have spears. | *gāras* = acc. pl. masc., regular *-as* ending |
| Ne hæfð hēo frēond. | She does not have a friend. | negation fronts *ne*, as with *Ne hæbbe ic sweord* |
| Wē habbaþ word. | We have a word. | *word* is neut., same form nom./acc. |

## Dative (indirect objects & prepositions)

| Old English | Modern English | Grammar note |
|---|---|---|
| Sē eorl rīdeþ tō þǣre byrig. | The earl rides to the city. | *tō* + dat.; *byrig* is the i-mutated dat. sg. of *burg* |
| Þā cyningas rīdaþ tō þǣm scipum. | The kings ride to the ships. | *þǣm* is dat. pl. for every gender |
| Se hlāford geaf þǣm eorle gold. | The lord gave the earl gold. | *þǣm eorle* (dat., indirect object) vs. *gold* (acc., direct object) |
| Þæt scip wæs on þǣre sǣ. | The ship was on the sea. | *on* + dat. for location (no motion) |
| Ic secge þǣm cnihte þæt word. | I say that word to the boy. | *þǣm cnihte* = dat. indirect object; *þæt word* = acc. direct object |
| Þæt cild sitteð on þǣm scipe. | The child sits on the ship. | *on* + dat. for location, as with *Þæt scip wæs on þǣre sǣ* |
| Se biscop geaf þǣm munuce bōc. | The bishop gave the monk a book. | *þǣm munuce* (dat.) vs. *bōc* (acc.), same pattern as *Se hlāford geaf þǣm eorle gold* |
| Hēo ēode fram þǣm hūse. | She went from the house. | *fram* + dat.; *ēode* is the irregular past of *gān* "to go" |

## Genitive (possession)

| Old English | Modern English | Grammar note |
|---|---|---|
| Þæs cyninges sweord is scearp. | The king's sword is sharp. | *þæs cyninges* = gen. sg. masc., "of the king" |
| Þǣre cwēne cild is glæd. | The queen's child is glad. | *þǣre cwēne* = gen. sg. fem. |
| Þæs munuces bōc is gōd. | The monk's book is good. | *þæs munuces* = gen. sg. masc., "of the monk" |
| Þæs cyninges dohtor is wīs. | The king's daughter is wise. | *þæs cyninges* = gen. sg. masc., as with *þæs cyninges sweord* |

## Verbs in action (present & past tense)

| Old English | Modern English | Grammar note |
|---|---|---|
| Ic sēo þone cyning. | I see the king. | *sēon* class V strong verb; *þone* = acc. masc. sg. |
| Þū lufast þā cwēne. | You love the queen. | *lufian* weak class II: *lufie, lufast, lufað* |
| Hēo secgð þæt word. | She says that word. | *secgan* is irregular weak |
| Þā menn feohtaþ wið þā fēondas. | The men fight against the enemies. | *mann* → pl. *menn* (i-mutation); *wið* + acc. |
| Þæt cild lufode þone catt. | The child loved the cat. | *lufode* = 3sg. past of *lufian* |
| Þā cild lufodon þone catt. | The children loved the cat. | *lufodon* = pl. past |
| Ic gehȳre þone fugol. | I hear the bird. | *gehȳran* weak class I: *gehȳre* (1sg), *gehȳrst*, *gehȳreð* |
| Hēo cymð tō þǣm hūse. | She comes to the house. | *cuman* irregular; 3sg. pres. = *cymð* (i-mutated) |
| Þā cild fundon þone catt. | The children found the cat. | *findan* class III strong: pl. past = *fundon* |
| Hēo seah þone hlāford. | She saw the lord. | *sēon* irregular; 3sg. past = *seah* |
| Þā menn druncon medu. | The men drank mead. | *drincan* class III strong: pl. past = *druncon* |
| Ic lufode þone hlāford. | I loved the lord. | *lufode* = 3sg. past of *lufian*, same pattern as *Þæt cild lufode þone catt* |

## A question with a number

| Old English | Modern English | Grammar note |
|---|---|---|
| Hū manige cyningas sind hēr? | How many kings are here? | *sind* = pl. present of *wesan* |
| Hū fela bēc hæfst þū? | How many books do you have? | *fela* + partitive noun; *hæfst* = 2sg. pres. of *habban* |
