# The Finnsburh Fragment

48 lines. Recounts part of the same Finn/Hengest episode alluded to later in
Beowulf, but survives independently: the sole manuscript leaf was
transcribed by George Hickes in 1705 and has since been lost, so Hickes's
copy is the only witness. Both the beginning and end are missing — the
fragment opens and closes mid-sentence. Old English text: sacred-texts.com,
Cook & Tinker, *Select Translations from Old English Poetry* (1902), public
domain. Modern English translation: own work for this repository, prose
rendering (not verse).

| 2 | Hnæf hleoðrode ða, heaðogeong cyning: | Then Hnæf spoke, the battle-young king: |
| 3 | "Ne ðis ne dagað eastan, ne her draca ne fleogeð, | "This is not dawn in the east, nor does a dragon fly here, |
| 4 | ne her ðisse healle hornas ne byrnað. | nor do this hall's gables burn here. |
| 5 | Ac her forð berað; fugelas singað, | But they advance; birds are singing, |
| 6 | gylleð græghama, guðwudu hlynneð, | the grey-coated one howls, battle-wood resounds, |
| 7 | scyld scefte oncwyð. Nu scyneð þes mona | shield answers shaft. Now this moon shines, |
| 8 | waðol under wolcnum. Nu arisað weadæda | wandering under the clouds. Now deeds of woe arise |
| 9 | ðe ðisne folces nið fremman willað. | that will carry out this people's hostility. |
| 10 | Ac onwacnigeað nu, wigend mine, | But awaken now, my warriors, |
| 11 | habbað eowre linda, hicgeaþ on ellen, | take up your shields, think on courage, |
| 12 | winnað on orde, wesað onmode!" | fight in the front rank, be resolute!" |
| 13 | ða aras mænig goldhladen ðegn, gyrde hine his swurde. | Then many a gold-laden thegn arose, girded himself with his sword. |
| 14 | ða to dura eodon drihtlice cempan, | Then to the doors went the lordly warriors, |
| 15 | Sigeferð and Eaha, hyra sword getugon, | Sigeferth and Eaha, drew their swords, |
| 16 | and æt oðrum durum Ordlaf and Guðlaf, | and at the other doors Ordlaf and Guthlaf, |
| 17 | and Hengest sylf hwearf him on laste. | and Hengest himself followed after them. |
| 18 | ða gyt Garulf Guðere styrde | Then still Guthere urged Garulf |
| 19 | ðæt he swa freolic feorh forman siðe | that he, so noble a life, on his first venture, |
| 20 | to ðære healle durum hyrsta ne bære, | should not carry his war-gear to the hall doors, |
| 21 | nu hyt niða heard anyman wolde, | now that one hardened in strife wished to take it, |
| 22 | ac he frægn ofer eal undearninga, | but he asked openly, before everyone, |
| 23 | deormod hæleð, hwa ða duru heolde. | the brave-minded warrior, who held the door. |
| 24 | "Sigeferð is min nama," cweð he, "ic eom Secgena leod, | "Sigeferth is my name," he said, "I am a prince of the Secgan, |
| 25 | wrecca wide cuð; fæla ic weana gebad, | an exile widely known; I have endured many woes, |
| 26 | heardra hilda. ðe is gyt her witod | hard battles. For you it is still decided here |
| 27 | swæðer ðu sylf to me secean wylle." | whichever outcome you yourself wish to seek from me." |
| 28 | ða wæs on healle wælslihta gehlyn; | Then there was in the hall the din of deadly blows; |
| 29 | sceolde cellod bord cenum on handa, | the curved shield had to, in the hand of the brave, |
| 30 | banhelm berstan (buruhðelu dynede), | see its rim burst (the hall-floor resounded), |
| 31 | oð æt ðære guðe Garulf gecrang, | until in that battle Garulf fell, |
| 32 | ealra ærest eorðbuendra, | first of all the earth's inhabitants, |
| 33 | Guðlafes sunu, ymbe hyne godra fæla, | Guthlaf's son, and around him many good men, |
| 34 | hwearflicra hræw. Hræfen wandrode, | their corpses lying about. The raven wandered, |
| 35 | sweart and sealobrun. Swurdleoma stod, | dark and dusky-brown. Sword-light glinted, |
