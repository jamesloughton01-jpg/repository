# Old English Metrical Charms (Selection)

Twelve short verse charms survive in Old English manuscripts, mixing
practical folk magic, herbal medicine, and Christian prayer — often in the
same charm. This is a selection of five of the best-known. Old English
text: sacred-texts.com, Cook & Tinker, *Select Translations from Old
English Poetry* (1902), public domain. Modern English translation: own
work for this repository, prose rendering (not verse). Each charm's prose
ritual instructions (who does what, when) are given as a note rather than
in the table, since they're prose rather than verse in the original.

## Wið Dweorh (Against a Dwarf)

Ritual instructions: take seven small wafers (of the kind used for
offerings), write the names Maximianus, Malchus, Iohannes, Martimianus,
Dionisius, Constantinus, and Serafion on each one. Sing the charm below
first into the patient's left ear, then the right ear, then over the top
of the head. A virgin then hangs it around the sufferer's neck; repeat for
three days.

| # | Old English | Modern English |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Her com in gangan, in spiderwiht, | Here came walking in, a spider-creature, |
| 2 | hæfde him his haman on handa, cwæð þæt þu his hæncgest wære, | it had its covering in its hand, said that you were its horse, |
| 3 | legde þe his teage an sweoran. Ongunnan him of þæm lande liþan; | laid its harness on your neck. They began to journey away from that land; |
| 4 | sona swa hy of þæm lande coman, þa ongunnan him ða liþu colian. | as soon as they left that land, then those limbs began to cool. |
| 5 | Þa com in gangan dweores sweostar; | Then the dwarf's sister came walking in; |
| 6 | þa geændade heo and aðas swor | then she made an end of it, and swore oaths |
| 7 | ðæt næfre þis ðæm adlegan derian ne moste, | that this could never harm the sick one, |
| 8 | ne þæm þe þis galdor begytan mihte, | nor him who might obtain this charm, |
| 9 | oððe þe þis galdor ongalan cuðe. | or him who knew how to chant this charm. |
| 10 | Amen. Fiaþ. | Amen. So be it. |

## Wið Færstice (For a Sudden Stitch)

Ritual instructions: boil feverfew, the red nettle that grows through a
building, and plantain, in butter. This charm — probably for rheumatic or
muscular pain — is the most famous of the collection, invoking both
Germanic "mighty women" hurling spears and Christian healing in the same
breath.

| # | Old English | Modern English |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hlude wæran hy, la, hlude, ða hy ofer þone hlæw ridan, | Loud they were, lo, loud, when they rode over the mound, |
| 2 | wæran anmode, ða hy ofer land ridan. | they were fierce-minded, when they rode over the land. |
| 3 | Scyld ðu ðe nu, þu ðysne nið genesan mote. | Shield yourself now, that you may survive this affliction. |
| 4 | Ut, lytel spere, gif her inne sie! | Out, little spear, if it be in here! |
| 5 | Stod under linde, under leohtum scylde, | It stood under a linden-shield, under a light shield, |
| 6 | þær ða mihtigan wif hyra mægen beræddon | where the mighty women mustered their power |
| 7 | and hy gyllende garas sændan; | and, screaming, sent their spears; |
| 8 | ic him oðerne eft wille sændan, | I will send another back to them in return, |
| 9 | fleogende flane forane togeanes. | a flying arrow, straight at them from the front. |
| 10 | Ut, lytel spere, gif hit her inne sy! | Out, little spear, if it be in here! |
| 11 | Sæt smið, sloh seax lytel, | A smith sat, forged a little knife, |
| 12 | iserna, wundrum swiðe. | of iron, wondrously sharp. |
| 13 | Ut, lytel spere, gif her inne sy! | Out, little spear, if it be in here! |
| 14 | Syx smiðas sætan, wælspera worhtan. | Six smiths sat, made slaughter-spears. |
| 15 | Ut, spere, næs in, spere! | Out, spear, not in, spear! |
| 16 | Gif her inne sy isernes dæl, | If there is a piece of iron in here, |
| 17 | hægtessan geweorc, hit sceal gemyltan. | the work of witches, it shall melt. |
| 18 | Gif ðu wære on fell scoten oððe wære on flæsc scoten | If you were shot in the skin, or shot in the flesh, |
| 19 | oððe wære on blod scoten | or shot in the blood, |
| 20 | oððe wære on lið scoten, næfre ne sy ðin lif atæsed; | or shot in the joint, may your life never be harmed; |
| 21 | gif hit wære esa gescot oððe hit wære ylfa gescot | if it was the shot of gods, or it was the shot of elves, |
| 22 | oððe hit wære hægtessan gescot, nu ic wille ðin helpan. | or it was the shot of a witch, now I will help you. |
| 23 | þis ðe to bote esa gescotes, ðis ðe to bote ylfa gescotes, | This is your remedy for the shot of gods, this for the shot of elves, |
| 24 | ðis ðe to bote hægtessan gescotes; ic ðin wille helpan. | this for the shot of a witch; I will help you. |
| 25 | Fleoh þær on fyrgenheafde. | Flee there, onto the mountain-top. |
| 26 | Hal westu, helpe ðin drihten! | Be well, may the Lord help you! |
| 27 | Nim þonne þæt seax, ado on wætan. | Then take that knife, put it into the liquid. |

## For Loss of Cattle

Mostly prose ritual rather than verse — a charm to be spoken toward each
compass direction in turn when livestock has gone missing, mixing Old
English with a repeated Latin formula (*Crux Christi ab oriente reducat*,
"may the cross of Christ bring it back from the east," and so on for
west, south, and north).

| # | Old English | Modern English |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Þonne þe mon ærest secge þæt þin ceap sy losod, þonne cweð þu ærest, ær þu elles hwæt cweþe: | When someone first tells you that your cattle are lost, say this first, before anything else: |
| 2 | Bæþleem hatte seo buruh þe Crist on acænned wæs, seo is gemærsod geond ealne middangeard; | "Bethlehem is the name of the town where Christ was born; it is renowned throughout all the world; |
| 3 | swa þyos dæd for monnum mære gewurþe þurh þa haligan Cristes rode! Amen. | so may this deed become renowned among men, through the holy cross of Christ!" Amen. |
| 4 | Gebide þe þonne þriwa east and cweþ þonne þriwa: *Crux Christi ab oriente reducat.* | Then bow three times to the east and say three times: *"May the cross of Christ bring it back from the east."* |
| 5 | Gebide þe þonne þriwa west and cweð þonne þriwa: *Crux Christi ab occidente reducat.* | Then bow three times to the west and say three times: *"May the cross of Christ bring it back from the west."* |
| 6 | Gebide þe þonne þriwa suð and cweþ þriwa: *Crux Christi ab austro reducat.* | Then bow three times to the south and say three times: *"May the cross of Christ bring it back from the south."* |
| 7 | Gebide þonne þriwa norð and cweð þriwa: *Crux Christi ab aquilone reducat, crux Christi abscondita est et inuenta est.* | Then bow three times to the north and say three times: *"May the cross of Christ bring it back from the north; the cross of Christ was hidden, and it was found."* |
| 8 | Iudeas Crist ahengon, dydon dæda þa wyrrestan, hælon þæt hy forhelan ne mihtan. | The Jews hanged Christ, did the worst of deeds, hid what they could not manage to hide. |
| 9 | Swa þeos dæd nænige þinga forholen ne wurþe þurh þa haligan Cristes rode. Amen. | So may this deed by no means remain hidden, through the holy cross of Christ. Amen. |

## For a Swarm of Bees

Ritual instructions: take earth, throw it with your right hand under your
right foot, and say the first part below; then, once the bees are
swarming, throw dust over them and say the second part.

| # | Old English | Modern English |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fo ic under fot, funde ic hit. | I seize it beneath my foot, I have found it. |
| 2 | Hwæt, eorðe mæg wið ealra wihta gehwilce | Listen, earth has power against every one of all creatures, |
| 3 | and wið andan and wið æminde | and against malice, and against forgetfulness, |
| 4 | and wið þa micelan mannes tungan. | and against the mighty tongue of man. |
| 5 | Sitte ge, sigewif, sigaþ to eorðan! | Settle down, victory-women, sink to the earth! |
| 6 | Næfre ge wilde to wuda fleogan. | Never fly away wild to the wood. |
| 7 | Beo ge swa gemindige mines godes, | Be as mindful of my welfare, |
| 8 | swa bið manna gehwilc metes and eþeles. | as every person is of food and home. |

## A Journey Charm

A prayer for protection on the road, invoking an unusually long roll-call
of biblical figures and, near the end, imagining the four Gospel-writers
as pieces of armor — Matthew a helmet, Mark a mail-coat, Luke a sword,
John a shield.

| # | Old English | Modern English |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ic me on þisse gyrde beluce and on godes helde bebeode | I enclose myself with this staff, and commit myself into God's protection, |
| 2 | wið þane sara stice, wið þane sara slege, | against the sore stitch, against the sore blow, |
| 3 | wið þane grymma gryre, | against the grim terror, |
| 4 | wið ðane micela egsa þe bið eghwam lað, | against the great dread that is hateful to everyone, |
| 5 | and wið eal þæt lað þe in to land fare. | and against all the evil that enters into the land. |
| 6 | Sygegealdor ic begale, sigegyrd ic me wege, | I chant a victory-charm, I carry a victory-staff, |
| 7 | wordsige and worcsige. Se me dege; | word-victory and work-victory. May that avail me; |
| 8 | ne me mere ne gemyrre, ne me maga ne geswence, | may no nightmare hinder me, nor hunger distress me, |
| 9 | ne me næfre minum feore forht ne gewurþe, | nor may I ever grow fearful for my life, |
| 10 | ac gehæle me ælmihtig and sunu and frofre gast, | but may the Almighty heal me, and the Son, and the comforting Spirit, |
| 11 | ealles wuldres wyrðig dryhten, | the Lord worthy of all glory, |
| 12 | swa swa ic gehyrde heofna scyppende. | just as I have heard of the Shaper of the heavens. |
| 13 | Abrame and Isace | Abraham and Isaac |
| 14 | and swilce men, Moyses and Iacob, | and such men, Moses and Jacob, |
| 15 | and Dauit and Iosep | and David and Joseph |
| 16 | and Evan and Annan and Elizabet, | and Eve and Anna and Elizabeth, |
| 17 | Saharie and ec Marie, modur Cristes, | Zechariah, and also Mary, mother of Christ, |
| 18 | and eac þæ gebroðru, Petrus and Paulus, | and also the brothers Peter and Paul, |
| 19 | and eac þusend þinra engla | and also a thousand of your angels — |
| 20 | clipige ic me to are wið eallum feondum. | I call on them for grace, against all enemies. |
| 21 | Hi me ferion and friþion and mine fore nerion, | May they carry me and protect me and preserve my journey, |
| 22 | eal me gehealdon, me gewealdon, | hold me wholly, govern me, |
| 23 | worces stirende; si me wuldres hyht, | directing my deeds; may there be for me the hope of glory, |
| 24 | hand ofer heafod, haligra rof, | a hand over my head, the glorious company of saints, |
| 25 | sigerofra sceolu, soðfæstra engla. | the host of the victory-glorious, of the righteous angels. |
| 26 | Biddu ealle bliðu mode | I pray to all, with a glad heart, |
| 27 | þæt me beo Matheus helm, Marcus byrne, | that Matthew be my helmet, Mark my mail-coat, |
| 28 | leoht, lifes rof, Lucos min swurd, | light, glory of life; Luke my sword, |
| 29 | scearp and scirecg, scyld Iohannes, | sharp and bright-edged; John my shield, |
| 30 | wuldre gewlitegod wælgar Serafhin. | gloriously adorned, the war-spear of the Seraphim. |
| 31 | Forð ic gefare, frind ic gemete, | Forth I journey, friends I shall meet, |
| 32 | eall engla blæd, eadiges lare. | all the glory of angels, the teaching of the blessed. |
| 33 | Bidde ic nu sigeres god godes miltse, | I now pray to the God of victory for God's mercy, |
| 34 | siðfæt godne, smylte and lihte | a good journey, gentle and easy |
| 35 | windas on waroþum. Windas gefran, | winds on the shores. I have known of winds, |
| 36 | circinde wæter simble gehælede | of swirling waters, always kept safe |
| 37 | wið eallum feondum. Freond ic gemete wið, | against all enemies. May I meet with a friend in this, |
| 38 | þæt ic on þæs ælmihtgian frið wunian mote, | that I may dwell in the peace of the Almighty, |
| 39 | belocun wið þam laþan, se me lyfes eht, | shut away from the hateful one, who hunts my life, |
| 40 | on engla blæd gestaþelod, | established in the glory of angels, |
| 41 | and inna halre hand heofna rices, | and within the whole hand of the kingdom of heaven, |
| 42 | þa hwile þe ic on þis life wunian mote. Amen. | for as long as I may dwell in this life. Amen. |
