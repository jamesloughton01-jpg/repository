# The Battle of Brunanburh

73 lines, embedded in the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle under the year 937 AD,
celebrating King Æthelstan and his brother Edmund's victory over a combined
force of Scots, Norse, and Britons. Old English text: *Bright's Anglo-Saxon
Reader* (James W. Bright), via Wikisource, CC BY-SA (underlying poem is
public domain, author unknown). Modern English translation: own work for
this repository, prose rendering (not verse).

| # | Old English | Modern English |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hēr Æðelstān cyning, eorla drihten, | In this year King Æthelstan, lord of warriors, |
| 2 | beorna bēahgifa, and his brōðor eac, | ring-giver of men, and his brother also, |
| 3 | Ēadmund æðeling, ealdorlangne tīr | Edmund the prince, won lifelong glory |
| 4 | geslōgon æt sæcce sweorda ęcgum | in battle with the edges of swords, |
| 5 | ymbe Brunanburh: bordweall clufon, | around Brunanburh: they split the shield-wall, |
| 6 | hēowon heaðolinde hamora lāfum, | hewed the battle-shields with the leavings of hammers, |
| 7 | eaforan Ēadweardes; swā him geæðele wæs | the sons of Edward; as it was innate to them, |
| 8 | fram cnēomāgum, ðæt hī æt campe oft | from their ancestors, that they in battle often |
| 9 | wið lāðra gehwæne land ealgodon, | defended their land against every enemy, |
| 10 | hord and hāmas. Hęttend crungon, | hoard and homes. The enemy perished, |
| 11 | Scotta leode and scipflotan, | the people of the Scots and the seafarers, |
| 12 | fǣge feollon: feld dennode | doomed, they fell: the field grew dark |
| 13 | sęcga swāte, siþþan sunne ūpp | with warriors' blood, from when the sun, up |
| 14 | on morgentīd, mǣre tungol, | in the morning-time, that glorious star, |
| 15 | glād ofer grundas, Godes candel beorht, | glided over the lands, God's bright candle, |
| 16 | ēces Drihtnes, oð sīo æðele gesceaft | the eternal Lord's, until that noble creation |
| 17 | sāh tō setle. Ðǣr læg sęcg mǫnig | sank to its setting. There lay many a warrior, |
| 18 | gārum āgēted, guma Norðerna | struck down by spears, a man of the Norse, |
| 19 | ofer scyld scoten, swylce Scyttisc ēac | shot over his shield, and a Scotsman too, |
| 20 | wērig wīges-sæd. Wesseaxe forð | weary, sated with battle. The West Saxons onward, |
| 21 | andlangne dæg ēoredcystum | the whole day long, in mounted troops, |
| 22 | on lāst lęgdon lāðum ðēodum; | pressed on the track of the hated people; |
| 23 | hēowon hęreflȳman hindan ðearle | hewed the fleeing army hard from behind |
| 24 | mēcum mylenscearpum. Myrce ne wyrndon | with mill-sharp swords. The Mercians did not refuse |
| 25 | heardes handplegan hæleða nānum, | hard hand-play to any of the warriors, |
| 26 | ðāra ðe mid Anlāfe ofer ēargebland | of those who with Anlaf, over the tossing sea, |
| 27 | on lides bōsme land gesōhton | sought the land in the ship's bosom, |
| 28 | fǣge tō gefeohte. Fīfe lāgon | doomed to the fight. Five lay |
| 29 | on ðām campstęde cyningas geonge | on that battlefield, young kings, |
| 30 | sweordum āswęfede, swylce seofone ēac | put to sleep by swords, and also seven |
| 31 | eorlas Anlāfes, unrīm hęrges, | earls of Anlaf, a countless host |
| 32 | flotena and Scotta. Ðǣr geflȳmed wearð | of seamen and Scots. There the Norse leader was put to flight, |
| 33 | Norðmanna brego, nēade gebǣded | driven by necessity, |
| 34 | tō lides stefne lȳtle weorode: | to a ship's prow with a small company: |
| 35 | crēad cnear on flot; cyning ūt gewāt | the ship pressed onto the sea; the king departed |
| 36 | on fealone flōd, feorh genęrede. | onto the dark flood, saved his life. |
| 37 | Swylce ðǣr ēac sē frōda mid flēame cōm | Likewise there also the old one came by flight |
| 38 | on his cȳððe norð, Constantīnus, | to his homeland in the north, Constantine, |
| 39 | hār hilderinc; hrēman ne ðorfte | the grey-haired warrior; he had no need to boast |
| 40 | mēca gemānon: he wæs his māga sceard, | of the meeting of swords: he was bereft of his kinsmen, |
| 41 | frēonda gefylled on folcstęde, | deprived of friends on the battlefield, |
| 42 | beslægen æt sæcce, and his sunu forlēt | struck down in the fight, and he left his son |
| 43 | on wælstōwe wundum forgrunden, | on the place of slaughter, ground down by wounds, |
| 44 | geongne æt gūðe. Gylpan ne ðorfte | young, in the battle. He had no need to boast, |
| 45 | beorn blandenfeax billgeslihtes, | the grey-haired man, of the sword-clash, |
| 46 | eald inwitta, nē Anlāf ðȳ mā | the old deceiver, nor did Anlaf any more, |
| 47 | mid heora hęrelāfum; hlihhan ne ðorfton, | with the remnants of their army; they had no cause to laugh |
| 48 | ðæt hī beaduweorca bęteran wurdon | that they were the better in deeds of battle |
| 49 | on campstęde cumbolgehnāstes; | on the battlefield, in the clash of banners, |
| 50 | gārmittinge, gumena gemōtes, | the meeting of spears, the encounter of men, |
| 51 | wǣpengewrīxles, ðæs hī on wælfelda | the exchange of weapons, which they on the slaughter-field |
| 52 | wið Ēadweardes eaforan plegodon. | played out against the sons of Edward. |
| 53 | Gewiton him þā Norðmęnn nægledcnearrum, | Then the Norsemen departed in their nailed ships, |
| 54 | drēorig daroða lāf, on Dinges mere | the sad remnant of spears, on Dingesmere, |
| 55 | ofer dēop wæter Dyflin sēcan, | over the deep water, to seek Dublin, |
| 56 | and eft Īraland, ǣwiscmōde. | and Ireland again, ashamed in spirit. |
| 57 | Swylce ðā gebrōðor bēgen aetsǫmne, | Likewise the two brothers together, |
| 58 | cyning and æðeling, cȳððe sōhton | king and prince, sought their homeland, |
| 59 | Wesseaxna land, wīges hrēmge. | the land of the West Saxons, exulting in war. |
| 60 | Lēton him behindan hrā bryttigan | They left behind them, to enjoy the corpses, |
| 61 | salowigpādan, ðone sweartan hræfn, | the dark-coated one, the black raven, |
| 62 | hyrnednębban, and ðone hasopādan | horny-beaked, and the dusky-coated one, |
| 63 | earn æftan hwīt, ǣses brūcan, | the white-tailed eagle, to enjoy the carrion, |
| 64 | grǣdigne gūðhafoc, and ðæt grǣge dēor, | the greedy war-hawk, and that grey beast, |
| 65 | wulf on wealda. Ne wearð wæl māre | the wolf in the forest. Nor was there ever a greater slaughter |
| 66 | on ðȳs īglande ǣfre gȳta | on this island, ever yet, |
| 67 | folces gefylled beforan ðyssum | of people felled, before this, |
| 68 | sweordes ęcgum, ðæs ðe ūs sęcgað bēc, | by the sword's edge, as the books tell us, |
| 69 | ealde ūðwitan, siþþan ēastan hider | the old scholars, since from the east hither |
| 70 | Ęngle and Seaxe ūpp becōmon | the Angles and Saxons came up, |
| 71 | ofer brāde brimu, Brytene sōhton, | over the broad seas, sought Britain, |
| 72 | wlance wīgsmiðas Wēalas ofercōmon, | proud war-smiths, overcame the Welsh, |
| 73 | eorlas ārhwate eard begēaton. | glory-eager earls, won the land. |
