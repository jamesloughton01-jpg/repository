# Resignation A & B

Preserved in the Exeter Book, folios 117b-119b. Despite the single
title given by early editors, modern scholarship treats this as two
unrelated poems, "Resignation A" and "Resignation B," joined only by
manuscript damage — a leaf is missing between them, and the poem breaks
off mid-sentence partway through B. **Resignation A** is a penitential
prayer: the speaker confesses sin and begs God for grace, patience, and
the strength to endure whatever trial is sent. **Resignation B** is a
wholly different poem in tone — an exile's lament, close in spirit to
The Wanderer and The Seafarer already in this collection, in which the
speaker, driven from his homeland and friendless, resolves to buy passage
on a ship and set out to sea, since he can no longer bear to stay. Old
English text: reconstructed from Benjamin Thorpe (ed. and tr.), *Codex
Exoniensis: A Collection of Anglo-Saxon Poetry* (London: Society of
Antiquaries, 1842), the first-ever printed edition of the Exeter Book —
public domain (Thorpe died in 1870; the edition is over 180 years old).
The scanned source uses a period Anglo-Saxon typeface that OCRs
unreliably, so individual letters were resolved using standard Old
English orthography, checked line-by-line against Thorpe's translation
only to confirm the intended sense — the Modern English column below is
an independent rendering in my own words, not adapted from his. Several
short manuscript gaps (marked `[...]`) follow Thorpe's own notes on
damage to the leaf.

## Resignation A

| # | Old English | Modern English |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Help min, þu halga dryhten, | Lord, holy one, lend me your aid — |
| 2 | þu gesceope heofon and eorðan | you who shaped both sky and ground |
| 3 | and wundor eall, min wuldor-cyning, | and every marvel in them, glorious King, |
| 4 | þe þæron sindon, ece dryhten, | everlasting Lord — |
| 5 | micel and manigfeald. | vast, uncountable wonders. |
| 6 | Ic þe, mære god, mine sawle bebeode, | Great God, I place my soul in your keeping, |
| 7 | and mines sylfes lic, and min word and min weorc, | this body, these words, everything I do, |
| 8 | wis dryhten, and eal min leoþu, | wise Lord — every limb of me — |
| 9 | leohtes hyrde. | you who keep the light. |
| 10 | And þa manigfealdan mine geþohtas | Even my tangled, restless thoughts, |
| 11 | getacna me, tungla hyrde, | point them somewhere, Keeper of the stars, |
| 12 | þær gelæst sy sawle minre | toward whatever is best for my soul |
| 13 | to gemearcianne meotudes willan, | in tracing out the Measurer's design, |
| 14 | þæt ic þe geþeo þinga gehwylce | so I prosper by you in every matter |
| 15 | and on me sylfum, soðcræft cyning, | and within myself too, King of true justice — |
| 16 | ræd araere. Þegn-þeof ne læte | build up good judgment in me. Don't let some thief in service to evil |
| 17 | on sceade sceþþan, þeah þe ic scyppendum, | do me harm in the dark, even though I've served my Maker |
| 18 | wuldor-cyninge, wacor hyrde, | half-heartedly — the King of glory — |
| 19 | wicum dryhtne, þonne min ræd wære. | the powerful Lord — worse than good sense demanded. |
| 20 | Forgif me to lisse, lifgende god, | Living God, out of kindness, |
| 21 | bitre bealodæde; ic þa bote gemon, | forgive my bitter, harmful acts; I haven't forgotten the price owed for them, |
| 22 | cyninga wuldor, cume to ðe gif ic mot. | Glory of kings, and I'd come to you, if I'm allowed. |
| 23 | Forgif þu me, min frea, fyrst and ondgiet, | Give me, my Lord, the time and the wits, |
| 24 | and geþyld and gemynd þinga gehwylces, | the patience, and the memory for whatever |
| 25 | þara þu me, soðfæst cyning, sendan wille | test you, just King, decide |
| 26 | to costunge. Nu þu const on me | to send my way. You already see |
| 27 | synna fela; feorma mec hwæðre, | how many sins are in me — care for me anyway, |
| 28 | meotod, for þinre miltse, | Measurer, out of your mercy, |
| 29 | þeah þe ic ma fremede firena gylta | even though I've committed worse crimes |
| 30 | þonne me god lyfde. | than God ever permitted. |
| 31 | Hæbbe ic þonne þearfe þæt ic þine, þeah, | So I need, all the same, to keep working |
| 32 | halge heofon-cyninges hyldo getilige | at earning the favor of heaven's holy King |
| 33 | lifgendum dagum, lif æfter oþrum | for as many days as I'm given — one life reaching for the next — |
| 34 | geseo and gesece, þæt me siþþan þær | seeking it out, so that afterward, there, |
| 35 | unne arfæst god ecan dreames, | the merciful God might grant me lasting joy, |
| 36 | lif alyfe, þeah þe lætlicor | might let me live on, even if I've been slow |
| 37 | bette bealodæde þonne bibodu wæron | in making up for my harmful deeds — slower than |
| 38 | halgan heofon-mægnes. | heaven's holy power commanded. |
| 39 | Hwæt, þu me her wela forgeafe... | And yet you've given me so much already here... |
| 40 | [...] | [...] |
| 41 | ...gesette minne hyht on þec, | ...I've pinned my hope on you, |
| 42 | forhte fore-þoncas, þæt hio fæstlice | these anxious plans of mine — let them |
| 43 | standan gestaðelad. Onget minne hige, | hold steady. Take my mind in hand, |
| 44 | gæsta god cyning, mid gearone ræd. | God, King of every spirit, with counsel ready-made. |
| 45 | Nu ic wundrige to þe, fæder mon-cynnes, | I'm hurrying toward you now, father of humankind, |
| 46 | of þisse worulde. Nu ic wat þæt ic sceal | away from this world. I know I'll need |
| 47 | ful unwyrðe feorma mec þonne | support, unworthy as I am, |
| 48 | wyrda waldend, in þine wuldor-dream, | Ruler of destinies, to reach your glorious joy — |
| 49 | and mec geleoran læt, leofra dryhten, | let me cross over, dearest Lord, |
| 50 | geoca minre gæste. | come to my spirit's aid. |
| 51 | Þonne is feonda to fela, æfestum eaden, | Enemies surround me, too many, bloated with spite — |
| 52 | hæbbe ic þonne æft freonda, þeah þe ic ær on lyfe lyt | may I still find friends afterward, even though in this life I've |
| 53 | earnode arna. Forlæt mec, englas þeah, | earned little kindness. Still, let angels |
| 54 | geniman on þinne neawest, nergende cyning, | carry me into your presence, saving King, |
| 55 | meotod, for þinre miltse, þeah þe ic mana fela | Measurer, for mercy's sake, though I've done |
| 56 | æfter dogrum dyde. | wrong again and again, day after day. |
| 57 | Ne læt þu mec næfre deofol seþeah | Whatever happens, never let the devil |
| 58 | þin lim læden on laðne sið, | drag a limb of yours down some hateful road, |
| 59 | þy læs hi on þone fore-þonc geseon motan, | in case that lets them see it as proof |
| 60 | þy þe hy him gylpu wel þuhten, | of what they've long boasted to themselves — |
| 61 | englas oferhydige, þonne ece Crist. | those arrogant angels — that they outrank eternal Christ. |
| 62 | Gelugon hy him æt þam geleafan; forþon hy lange sculon, | They fooled only themselves with that belief; so for a long age |
| 63 | wræge wihta, wræce þrowian. | those cursed creatures must live out their banishment. |
| 64 | Forscild þu mec and gescyld him, | Defend me, and keep them off me, |
| 65 | þonne storm cyme minum gæste ongean; | whenever a storm turns against my spirit; |
| 66 | geoca þonne, mihtig dryhten, minre sawle, | come to my soul's rescue then, mighty Lord — |
| 67 | gescyld hyre and geferma hy. | shelter it, keep it fed. |
| 68 | Fæder mon-cynnes, hafa gehogod, | Father of humankind, hold it in mind, |
| 69 | hæl, ece god, meotod, mihtum swiþ. | keep it safe, everlasting God, Measurer strong beyond measure. |

## Resignation B

| # | Old English | Modern English |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Min is nu þa mod-sefa synnum fah, | My heart carries the stain of sin now, |
| 2 | and ic ymb sawle eom wearn-sið forht, | and I dread the perilous road ahead for my soul, |
| 3 | þeah þu me wela sealde, ara on þisse eorþan; | even though you gave me plenty, blessing after blessing on this earth; |
| 4 | þe sie ealles þonc, meorda and miltsa, | thank you for every one of them — the rewards, the mercies — |
| 5 | þara þu me fealde. No þær earnunga | that you handed me. None of it was earned; |
| 6 | ænige wæron mid; hwæðre ic me ealles þæs | I did nothing to deserve any of it. And yet, in spite of that, |
| 7 | ellen wylle habban and hlyhhan, | I mean to keep my courage and laugh, |
| 8 | and me hyhtan to, wræcsian mec on forð-weg, | to keep hoping, to brace myself for the road ahead, |
| 9 | and wundian sylf to þam siþe þe ic ongean sceal, | to steel myself for the journey now facing me, |
| 10 | gæst gearwian, and me þæt eal for gode | to make my spirit ready, and bear all of it gladly, |
| 11 | þolian bliþe mode. Nu ic gebunden eom | for God's sake. I'm bound tight now, |
| 12 | fæste in minum ferþe, hwæþre me frea wite | deep inside myself — though may the Lord still correct |
| 13 | sume þara synna þe ic me sylf ne conn | whichever of my sins I can't even |
| 14 | onwritan gleawlice. Gode ic hæbbe | name clearly myself. I've angered God — |
| 15 | abolgen, dryhten mon-cynnes; forþon ic þus bitre wear | the Lord of all people — and that's why I was struck down so hard, |
| 16 | geswencet fore þisse worulde, swa min geswinc wæron | in front of the world, to match how badly I'd behaved — |
| 17 | micle fore monnum, þæt ic martyrdom | badly enough, in plain view of everyone, that what I'm enduring |
| 18 | beorne aþreoðe. Ne eom ic bealdra gleaw, | feels like a martyr's suffering. I'm no confident, clever judge, |
| 19 | wis fore werode, forþon ic þas word sprece | no wise voice before a crowd — that's why these are the words I speak, |
| 20 | þus on ferþe, swa me on fyrste gelamp | this is how it sits in my mind, since hardship found me |
| 21 | yrmþu ofer eorþan; þæt ic a þolode | from the very start of my time on earth; I went without |
| 22 | geara gehwylce godes ealles, | every good thing, year after year, |
| 23 | þonc-mod earfoþa ma þonne on oþrum, | dwelling on my troubles more than most people do, |
| 24 | fyrhto in folce; forþon ic afysed eom, | afraid among my own people; that's why I've been pushed out, |
| 25 | earm of minre eðle. Ne mæg þæs anhoga, | poor, from my own homeland. A man alone like this can't, |
| 26 | leod-wynna leas, leng þrowian, | cut off from the joys of belonging, hold out any longer — |
| 27 | winelas wræcca; is him wa-metod, | a friendless exile, with fate itself turned against him; |
| 28 | gnornaþ on his geoguðe, and him æghwylce | he mourns while still young, and at every turn |
| 29 | [...] men fullestað, ycað his yrmþu, | [...] no one lends a hand — his troubles only pile higher — |
| 30 | and he þæt eal þolað, sar-cwide secga, | and he takes it all, the cruel talk of others, |
| 31 | and he bið a þegna geomor, mod morgen-seoc. | his spirits forever low, sick at heart come every morning. |
| 32 | Ic bi me cuðlicost secge þis sorh-spell, | No one can tell this sad story better than I can, about myself — |
| 33 | and ymb sið sprece, longunge suð, | I'll speak of the journey, this pull southward, |
| 34 | and on lagu þence. Nat min [...] | and turn my thoughts to the sea. My [...] has no idea |
| 35 | [...] hwy ic bycge bat on gearwe, | [...] why I'd buy a boat, fitted out and ready, |
| 36 | sceoc on stroðe. Nah ic feala goldes, | swift there on the shore. I don't have much gold, |
| 37 | ne huru þæs freonda þe me gefylste | and certainly not many friends who'd back me |
| 38 | to þam wið-race. Nu ic me sylf ne mæg | on that crossing. I can't, on my own, |
| 39 | fore minum won-æhtum willan aþreotan; | see my wish through — I own too little for that; |
| 40 | wudu mot him weaxan, wyrde bidan, | the timber can keep growing where it stands, wait out its fate, |
| 41 | cnearum lædan. Ic for cealde ne mæg | before it's ever shaped into a ship. And in this coldness I can't |
| 42 | ænigne mon-cynnes mode gelufian, | bring myself to care for anyone, |
| 43 | eorl on eðle. Eala, dryhten min, | not one noble soul in this land. Oh, my Lord, |
| 44 | mihtig mund-bora, þæs ic eom mode seoc, | mighty guardian, my mind is so sick over this, |
| 45 | bitre abolgen. Is seo bot æt þe | so bitterly troubled. Yet the cure lies with you, |
| 46 | gelong, æþelinga wyn; ic on leohte ne mæg | Joy of princes — I can't stay in the light, |
| 47 | butan earfoþum ænige hwile, | not even briefly, without hardship weighing on me, |
| 48 | fea-sceaft hæle. [...] | destitute as I am. [...] |
| 49 | þonne ic me to freme geþeode hæfde... | back when I had once turned to you for help... |
