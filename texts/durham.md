# Durham

21 lines. Composed c. 1104-1109 AD, celebrating the city of Durham and the
relics housed in its cathedral shortly after St Cuthbert's body was moved
there. One of the very last poems written in the classical Old English
alliterative style, right at the edge of the Old English/Middle English
transition. Old English text: sacred-texts.com, Cook & Tinker, *Select
Translations from Old English Poetry* (1902), public domain. Modern
English translation: own work for this repository, prose rendering (not
verse).

| # | Old English | Modern English |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is þeos burch breome geond Breotenrice, | This city is renowned throughout Britain, |
| 2 | steppa gestaðolad, stanas ymbutan | its terraces founded, with stones round about, |
| 3 | wundrum gewæxen. Weor ymbeornad, | wondrously grown. The Wear flows around it, |
| 4 | ea yðum stronge, and ðer inne wunað | a river strong with waves, and therein dwell |
| 5 | feola fisca kyn on floda gemonge. | many kinds of fish amid the currents. |
| 6 | And ðær gewexen is wudafæstern micel; | And there has grown a great fortress of woodland; |
| 7 | wuniad in ðem wycum wilda deor monige, | many wild animals dwell in those retreats, |
| 8 | in deope dalum deora ungerim. | countless beasts in deep valleys. |
| 9 | Is in ðere byri eac bearnum gecyðed | It is also made known to the people, in that city, |
| 10 | ðe arfesta eadig Cudberch | the pious, blessed Cuthbert, |
| 11 | and ðes clene cyninges heafud, | and the pure king's head, |
| 12 | Osuualdes, Engle leo, and Aidan biscop, | Oswald's — lion of the English — and Bishop Aidan, |
| 13 | Eadberch and Eadfrið, æðele geferes. | Eadberht and Eadfrith, noble companions. |
| 14 | Is ðer inne midd heom æðelwold biscop | Within, among them, is Bishop Æthelwold, |
| 15 | and breoma bocera Beda, and Boisil abbot, | and Bede, famous among scholars, and Abbot Boisil, |
| 16 | ðe clene Cudberte on gecheðe | who eagerly taught the pure Cuthbert |
| 17 | lerde lustum, and he his lara wel genom. | in his youth, and he learned his teaching well. |
| 18 | Eardiæð æt ðem eadige in in ðem minstre | There dwell, with that blessed one, in that minster, |
| 19 | unarimeda reliquia, | countless relics, |
| 20 | ðær monia wundrum gewurðaþ, ðes ðe writ seggeð, | where many are wondrously honored, as writing tells, |
| 21 | midd ðene drihnes wer domes bideð. | together with that man of the Lord, awaiting Judgment. |
