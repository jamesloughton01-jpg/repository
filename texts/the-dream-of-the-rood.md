# The Dream of the Rood

Preserved in the 10th-century Vercelli Book; may be considerably older. Old
English text: Wikisource, CC BY-SA (underlying poem is public domain, author
unknown). Modern English translation: own work for this repository, prose
rendering (not verse).

| # | Old English | Modern English |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hwæt! Ic swefna cyst secgan wylle, | Listen! I wish to tell the best of dreams, |
| 2 | hwæt mē gemǣtte tō midre nihte, | what came to me in a dream at midnight, |
| 3 | syðþan reordberend reste wunedon. | after speech-bearers had settled to rest. |
| 4 | Þūhte mē þæt ic gesāwe syllicre trēow | It seemed to me that I saw a wondrous tree |
| 5 | on lyft lǣdan, lēohte bewunden, | lifted into the air, wound about with light, |
| 6 | bēama beorhtost. Eall þæt bēacen wæs | brightest of trees. That beacon was entirely |
| 7 | begoten mid golde; gimmas stōdon | covered with gold; gems stood |
| 8 | fægere æt foldan scēatum, swylce þǣr fīfe wǣron | fair at the corners of the earth, and there were five likewise |
| 9 | uppe on þām eaxlgespanne. Behēoldon þǣr engel dryhtnes ealle | up on the cross-beam. All the angels of the Lord beheld it there, |
| 10 | fægere þurh forðgesceaft; ne wæs ðǣr hūru fracodes gealga, | fair through eternity; it was no criminal's gallows there, |
| 11 | ac hine þǣr behēoldon hālige gāstas, | but holy spirits beheld it there, |
| 12 | men ofer moldan, ond eall þēos mǣre gesceaft. | men over the earth, and all this glorious creation. |
| 13 | Syllic wæs se sigebēam, ond ic synnum fāh, | Wondrous was that tree of victory, and I, stained with sins, |
| 14 | forwundod mid wommum. Geseah ic wuldres trēow | badly wounded with blemishes. I saw the tree of glory |
| 15 | wǣdum geweorðode, wynnum scīnan, | honored with garments, shining with joys, |
| 16 | gegyred mid golde; gimmas hæfdon | adorned with gold; gems had |
| 17 | bewrigene weorðlīce Wealdendes trēow. | worthily covered the Ruler's tree. |
| 18 | Hwæðre ic þurh þæt gold ongytan meahte | Yet through that gold I could perceive |
| 19 | earmra ǣrgewin, þæt hit ǣrest ongan | an ancient suffering, that it first began |
| 20 | swǣtan on þā swīðran healfe. Eall ic wæs mid sorgum gedrēfed. | to bleed on the right side. I was entirely troubled with sorrows. |
| 21 | forht ic wæs for þǣre fægran gesyhðe. Geseah ic þæt fūse bēacen | I was afraid because of that fair sight. I saw that shifting beacon |
| 22 | wendan wǣdum ond blēom; hwīlum hit wæs mid wǣtan bestēmed, | change its covering and colors; at times it was drenched with wetness, |
| 23 | beswyled mid swātes gange, hwīlum mid since gegyrwed. | soaked with the flow of blood, at times adorned with treasure. |
| 24 | Hwæðre ic þǣr licgende lange hwīle | Yet lying there for a long while |
| 25 | behēold hrēowcearig Hǣlendes trēow, | I, sorrowful, beheld the Savior's tree, |
| 26 | oð ðæt ic gehȳrde þæt hit hlēoðrode; | until I heard that it spoke; |
| 27 | ongan þā word sprecan wudu sēlesta: | the best of woods then began to speak words: |
| 28 | "Þæt wæs gēara iū, – ic þæt gȳta geman – | "That was long ago – I still remember it – |
| 29 | þæt ic wæs āhēawen holtes on ende, | that I was cut down at the edge of the forest, |
| 30 | āstyred of stefne mīnum. Genāman mē ðǣr strange fēondas, | moved from my trunk. Strong enemies took me there, |
| 31 | geworhton him þǣr tō wǣfersȳne, hēton mē heora wergas hebban; | made me into a spectacle for themselves, ordered me to lift up their criminals; |
| 32 | bǣron mē ðǣr beornas on eaxlum, oð ðæt hīe mē on beorg āsetton, | men carried me there on their shoulders, until they set me on a hill, |
| 33 | gefæstnodon mē þǣr fēondas genōge. Geseah ic þā Frēan mancynnes | enough enemies fastened me there. Then I saw the Lord of mankind |
| 34 | efstan elne micle, þæt hē mē wolde on gestīgan. | hasten with great courage, wishing to climb upon me. |
| 35 | Þǣr ic þā ne dorste ofer Dryhtnes word | There I did not dare, against the Lord's word, |
| 36 | būgan oððe berstan, þā ic bifian geseah | to bow or break, when I saw |
| 37 | eorðan scēatas. Ealle ic mihte | the surfaces of the earth tremble. I could have |
| 38 | fēondas gefyllan, hwæðre ic fæste stōd. | felled all his enemies, yet I stood fast. |
| 39 | Ongyrede hine þā geong hæleð, – þæt wæs God ælmihtig! – | The young hero then stripped himself – that was God almighty! – |
| 40 | strang ond stīðmōd. Gestāh hē on gealgan hēanne, | strong and resolute. He climbed onto the high gallows, |
| 41 | mōdig on manigra gesyhðe, þā hē wolde mancyn lȳsan. | brave in the sight of many, since he wished to redeem mankind. |
| 42 | Bifode ic þā mē se beorn ymbclypte. Ne dorste ic hwæðre bugan to eorðan, | I trembled when the man embraced me. Yet I dared not bow to the ground, |
| 43 | feallan tō foldan scēatum, ac ic sceolde fæste standan. | fall to the earth's surface, but I had to stand fast. |
| 44 | Rōd wæs ic ārǣred; āhōf ic rīcne Cyning, | I was raised up as a cross; I lifted up the mighty King, |
| 45 | heofona Hlāford; hyldan mē ne dorste. | the Lord of heaven; I dared not bend. |
| 46 | Þurhdrifan hī mē mid deorcan næglum; on mē syndon þā dolg gesīene, | They drove dark nails through me; on me those wounds are visible, |
| 47 | opene inwidhlemmas; ne dorste ic hira ǣnigum sceððan. | open malicious wounds; I dared not harm any of them. |
| 48 | Bysmeredon hīe unc būtū ætgædere. Eall ic wæs mid blōde bestēmed, | They mocked the two of us together. I was entirely drenched with blood, |
| 49 | begoten of þæs guman sīdan, siððan hē hæfde his gāst onsended. | poured from the man's side, after he had sent forth his spirit. |
| 50 | Feala ic on þām beorge gebiden hæbbe | I have endured many things on that hill, |
| 51 | wrāðra wyrda: geseah ic weruda God | cruel fates: I saw the God of hosts |
| 52 | þearle þenian. Þȳstro hæfdon | stretched out harshly. Darkness had |
| 53 | bewrigen mid wolcnum Wealdendes hrǣw, | covered with clouds the Ruler's corpse, |
| 54 | scīrne scīman, sceadu forð ēode, | the bright radiance; a shadow went forth, |
| 55 | wann under wolcnum. Wēop eal gesceaft, | dark under the clouds. All creation wept, |
| 56 | cwīðdon cyninges fyll: Crīst wæs on rōde. | lamented the king's fall: Christ was on the cross. |
| 57 | Hwæðere þǣr fūse feorran cwōman | Yet eager ones came there from afar |
| 58 | tō þām Æðelinge; ic þæt eall behēold. | to the noble one; I beheld all of that. |
| 59 | Sāre ic wæs mid sorgum gedrēfed, hnāg ic hwæðre þām secgum tō handa, | I was sorely troubled with sorrows, yet I bowed to the hands of the men, |
| 60 | ēaðmōd elne mycle. Genāmon hīe þǣr ælmihtigne god, | humbly, with great courage. They took almighty God there, |
| 61 | āhōfon hine of ðām hefian wīte; forlēton mē þā hilderincas | lifted him from that heavy torment; the warriors then abandoned me, |
| 62 | standan stēame bedrifenne; eall ic wæs mid strǣlum forwundod. | standing, drenched with moisture; I was entirely wounded with spear-shafts. |
| 63 | Ālēdon hīe ðǣr limwērigne, gestōdon him æt his līces hēafdum; | They laid him down there, limb-weary; they stood at the head of his body; |
| 64 | behēoldon hīe ðǣr heofenes Dryhten, ond hē hine ðǣr hwīle reste, | they beheld there the Lord of heaven, and he rested there a while, |
| 65 | mēðe æfter ðām miclan gewinne. Ongunnon him þā moldern wyrcan | weary after that great struggle. They began then to build him an earth-house, |
| 66 | beornas on banan gesyhðe; curfon hīe ðæt of beorhtan stāne, | men, in the sight of his slayer; they carved it out of bright stone, |
| 67 | gesetton hīe ðǣron sigora Wealdend. Ongunnon him þā sorhlēoð galan | they set the Ruler of victories therein. They began then to sing a sorrow-song for him, |
| 68 | earme on þā ǣfentīde, þā hīe woldon eft sīðian | wretched, in the evening-tide, when they wished to journey back, |
| 69 | mēðe fram þām mǣran Þēodne; reste hē ðǣr mǣte werode. | weary, from that glorious Prince; he rested there with a small company. |
| 70 | Hwæðere wē ðǣr grēotende gōde hwīle | Yet we, weeping, for a good while |
| 71 | stōdon on staðole, stefn up gewāt | stood in our place, [as] a voice went up |
| 72 | hilderinca; hrǣw cōlode, | from the warriors; the corpse grew cold, |
| 73 | fæger feorgbold. Þā ūs man fyllan ongan | the fair body-house. Then men began to fell us |
| 74 | ealle tō eorðan; þæt wæs egeslic wyrd! | all to the ground; that was a terrible fate! |
| 75 | Bedealf ūs man on dēopan sēaþe; hwæðre mē þǣr Dryhtnes þegnas, | Men buried us in a deep pit; yet there the Lord's thegns, |
| 76 | frēondas gefrūnon, | friends, discovered me, |
| 77 | gyredon mē golde ond seolfre. | adorned me with gold and silver. |
| 78 | Nū ðū miht gehȳran, hæleð mīn se lēofa, | Now you may hear, my beloved man, |
| 79 | þæt ic bealuwara weorc gebiden hæbbe | that I have endured the work of evildoers, |
| 80 | sārra sorga. Is nū sǣl cumen | grievous sorrows. Now the time has come |
| 81 | þæt mē weorðiað wīde and sīde | that, far and wide, |
| 82 | menn ofer moldan and eall þēos mǣre gesceaft, | men over the earth, and all this glorious creation, honor me, |
| 83 | gebiddaþ him tō þyssum bēacne. On mē Bearn Godes | pray to this beacon. On me the Son of God |
| 84 | þrōwode hwīle; for þan ic þrymfæst nū | suffered for a time; therefore, glorious, |
| 85 | hlīfige under heofenum, and ic hǣlan mæg | I now tower under the heavens, and I am able to heal |
| 86 | ǣghwylcne ānra þāra þe him bið egesa tō mē. | each one of those who feels awe toward me. |
| 87 | Iū ic wæs geworden wīta heardost, | Once I became the harshest of torments, |
| 88 | lēodum lāðost, ǣr þan ic him līfes weg | most hateful to people, before I opened to them |
| 89 | rihtne gerȳmde, reordberendum. | the right way of life, for speech-bearers. |
| 90 | Hwæt, mē þā geweorþode wuldres Ealdor | Lo, the Prince of glory honored me then |
| 91 | ofer holmwudu, heofonrīces Weard, | above all forest-trees, the Guardian of the heavenly kingdom, |
| 92 | swylce swā hē his mōdor ēac, Marīan sylfe, | just as he also honored his mother, Mary herself, |
| 93 | ælmihtig God for ealle menn | almighty God, above all |
| 94 | geweorðode ofer eall wīfa cynn. | women, honored her above all womankind. |
| 95 | Nū ic þē hāte, hæleð mīn se lēofa, | Now I command you, my beloved man, |
| 96 | þæt ðū þās gesyhðe secge mannum, | that you tell this vision to men, |
| 97 | onwrēoh wordum þæt hit is wuldres bēam, | reveal with words that it is the tree of glory, |
| 98 | se ðe ælmihtig god on þrōwode | on which almighty God suffered |
| 99 | for mancynnes manegum synnum | for mankind's many sins |
| 100 | ond Adomes ealdgewyrhtum. | and for Adam's ancient deeds. |
| 101 | Dēað hē þǣr byrigde, hwæðere eft dryhten ārās | Death he tasted there, yet the Lord rose again |
| 102 | mid his miclan mihte mannum tō helpe. | with his great might, to help mankind. |
| 103 | Hē ðā on heofenas āstāg. Hider eft fundaþ | He then ascended into the heavens. He will come again to this place, |
| 104 | on þysne middangeard mancynn sēcan | to this middle-earth, to seek mankind, |
| 105 | on dōmdæge Dryhten sylfa, | on doomsday, the Lord himself, |
| 106 | ælmihtig God and his englas mid, | almighty God, and his angels with him, |
| 107 | þæt hē þonne wile dēman, se āh dōmes geweald, | so that he will then judge — he who holds power over judgment — |
| 108 | ānra gehwylcum swā hē him ǣrur hēr | each one, according to how he himself earlier here, |
| 109 | on þyssum lǣnum līfe geearnaþ. | in this transitory life, has earned. |
| 110 | Ne mæg þǣr ǣnig unforht wesan | No one may be unafraid there |
| 111 | for þām worde þe se Wealdend cwyð. | because of the word which the Ruler speaks. |
| 112 | frīneð hē for þǣre mænige hwǣr se man sīe, | He will ask, before the multitude, where the man may be |
| 113 | se ðe for Dryhtnes naman dēaðes wolde | who, for the Lord's name, was willing to taste |
| 114 | biteres onbyrigan, swā hē ǣr on ðām bēame dyde. | bitter death, as he had done earlier on the cross. |
| 115 | Ac hīe þonne forhtiað, ond fēa þencaþ | But then they will be afraid, and few will think |
| 116 | hwæt hīe tō Crīste cweðan onginnen. | what they might begin to say to Christ. |
| 117 | Ne þearf ðǣr þonne ǣnig anforht wesan | No one need be afraid there then |
| 118 | þe him ǣr in brēostum bereð bēacna sēlest, | who bears beforehand in his breast the best of beacons, |
| 119 | ac ðurh ðā rōde sceal rīce gesēcan | but through the cross every soul must seek |
| 120 | of eorðwege ǣghwylc sāwl, | the kingdom from the earthly path, |
| 121 | sēo þe mid Wealdende wunian þenceð." | that which intends to dwell with the Ruler." |
| 122 | Gebæd ic mē þā tō þām bēame blīðe mōde, | I then prayed to that tree with a glad mind, |
| 123 | elne mycle, þǣr ic āna wæs | with great zeal, where I was alone |
| 124 | mǣte werede. Wæs mōdsefa | with little company. My spirit was |
| 125 | āfȳsed on forðwege, feala ealra gebād | impelled on the journey forth; I have endured many |
| 126 | langunghwīla. Is mē nū līfes hyht | hours of longing. Now it is my life's hope |
| 127 | þæt ic þone sigebēam sēcan mōte | that I may seek that tree of victory, |
| 128 | āna oftor þonne ealle men | alone, more often than all men, |
| 129 | well weorþian. Mē is willa tō ðām | and honor it well. My desire for that is |
| 130 | mycel on mōde, and mīn mundbyrd is | great in my mind, and my protection is |
| 131 | geriht tō þǣre rōde. Nāh ic rīcra feala | directed toward the cross. I have not many powerful |
| 132 | frēonda on foldan, ac hīe forð heonon | friends on earth, but they have departed hence, |
| 133 | gewiton of worulde drēamum, sōhton him wuldres Cyning, | from the joys of the world, sought the King of glory; |
| 134 | lifiaþ nū on heofenum mid Hēahfædere, | they live now in heaven with the High Father, |
| 135 | wuniaþ on wuldre; and ic wēne mē | dwell in glory; and I hope for myself, |
| 136 | daga gehwylce hwænne mē Dryhtnes rōd, | each day, for when the Lord's cross, |
| 137 | þe ic hēr on eorðan ǣr scēawode, | which I here on earth beheld before, |
| 138 | on þysson lǣnan līfe gefetige | will fetch me from this transitory life |
| 139 | ond mē þonne gebringe þǣr is blis mycel, | and then bring me where there is great bliss, |
| 140 | drēam on heofonum, þǣr is dryhtnes folc | joy in heaven, where the Lord's people |
| 141 | geseted tō symle, þǣr is singāl blis, | are seated at the feast, where there is perpetual bliss, |
| 142 | ond mē þonne āsette þǣr ic syþþan mōt | and then set me where I may afterward |
| 143 | wunian on wuldre, well mid þām hālgum | dwell in glory, well among the saints, |
| 144 | drēames brūcan. Sī mē Dryhten frēond, | enjoy joy. May the Lord be a friend to me, |
| 145 | se ðe hēr on eorþan ǣr þrōwode | he who here on earth suffered before |
| 146 | on þām gealgtrēowe for guman synnum. | on the gallows-tree for men's sins. |
| 147 | hē ūs onlȳsde ond ūs līf forgeaf, | He redeemed us and gave us life, |
| 148 | heofonlicne hām. Hiht wæs genīwad | a heavenly home. Hope was renewed, |
| 149 | mid blēdum and mid blisse þām þe þǣr bryne þolodan. | with blessings and with bliss, for those who had endured burning there. |
| 150 | Se Sunu wæs sigorfæst on þām sīðfate, | The Son was victorious on that expedition, |
| 151 | mihtig and spēdig, þā hē mid manigeo cōm, | mighty and successful, when he came with a multitude, |
| 152 | gāsta weorode, on Godes rīce, | a host of souls, into God's kingdom, |
| 153 | Anwealda ælmihtig, englum tō blisse | the almighty Ruler, to the joy of angels |
| 154 | and eallum ðām hālgum þām þe on heofonum ǣr | and to all the saints who, before, in heaven, |
| 155 | wunedon on wuldre, þā heora Wealdend cwōm, | dwelt in glory, when their Ruler came, |
| 156 | ælmihtig God, þær his ēðel wæs. | almighty God, where his homeland was. |
