# Bede's Death Song

Five lines, in the Northumbrian dialect, said to have been composed by the
Venerable Bede on his deathbed in 735 AD — among the oldest attributed Old
English poems. Survives in dozens of manuscript copies across Europe. Old
English text (Northumbrian version): sacred-texts.com, Cook & Tinker,
*Select Translations from Old English Poetry* (1902), public domain. Modern
English translation: own work for this repository.

| # | Old English | Modern English |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fore thaem neidfaerae naenig uuiurthit | Before that inevitable journey, no one becomes |
| 2 | thoncsnotturra, than him tharf sie | wiser in thought than he has need to be, |
| 3 | to ymbhycggannae aer his hiniongae | to consider, before his going hence, |
| 4 | huaet his gastae godaes aeththa yflaes | what will be judged to his spirit — of good or evil — |
| 5 | aefter deothdaege doemid uueorthae. | after his day of death. |
