# The Proverbs of Alfred

**A different stage of the language.** Unlike everything else in this
repository, this text is classified by scholars as **Early Middle
English, not Old English** — composed roughly 1150-1180, probably in
Sussex, a full century after the Norman Conquest. It's included here for
context and comparison (it shows what English looked like one step past
the texts elsewhere in this collection), not as an Old English text
proper. A collection of gnomic wisdom sayings — popular proverbs,
religious instruction, advice on marriage and behavior — attributed to
King Alfred the Great, who had already become a legendary wise-king
figure by this period; there's no real evidence he composed any of it.

**Only a short excerpt.** Four 13th-century manuscripts survive (British
Library Cotton Galba A.xix, Maidstone Museum A.13, Trinity College
Cambridge B.14.39, and Jesus College Oxford 29), each with different
wording, and the full collection runs to several hundred lines. Skeat's
1907 critical edition prints all four in parallel with heavy line-by-line
apparatus rather than one continuous text, which makes reliably extracting
a longer clean passage error-prone. Rather than risk a mixed-up or
misread transcription, this collection includes only the 16-line
Prologue, taken from a single manuscript witness (the Cotton Galba text,
called "B" in Skeat's edition) with its readings resolved but not
mixed with the other three manuscripts. Old English/Early Middle English
text: Walter W. Skeat (ed.), *The Proverbs of Alfred, Re-edited from the
Manuscripts* (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1907), public domain (Skeat died
in 1912; the edition is well over a century old). Modern English
translation: own work for this repository, prose rendering (not verse).
