# The Whale

88 lines, preserved in the Exeter Book. One of three Old English animal
poems drawing on the *Physiologus* tradition (a Greek-derived Christian
bestiary that read animal behavior as moral allegory), alongside The
Panther and The Partridge — not included in this collection. Describes
the whale, here named Fastitocalon, whose enormous back is mistaken by
sailors for an island; they anchor and light a fire on it, at which point
the whale dives, drowning them. A second section describes the whale
luring smaller fish into its mouth with a sweet scent. Both are read as
allegories for the devil, who lures souls with false comfort before
dragging them down to hell. Old English text: Grein (founder) and
Wülcker (reviser), *Bibliothek der angelsächsischen Poesie*, Band III,
Heft 1 (Kassel: Georg H. Wigand, 1897), a public-domain scholarly edition
(over 125 years old; the same edition already used for this collection's
Exeter Book Riddles selection). Modern English translation: own work for
this repository, prose rendering (not verse).
