# The Husband's Message

Preserved in the Exeter Book, folio 123a, immediately before The Ruin.
Often read as a companion piece to *The Wife's Lament* (see the Texts
dropdown above), though the connection is debated. The speaker is usually taken to be a
rune-inscribed piece of wood — perhaps a staff or tally-stick — carrying a
message from an exiled man to his wife or betrothed, urging her to sail
south and rejoin him. The same manuscript damage that affects the
following poem, The Ruin, has destroyed parts of this text too; those
gaps are marked `[...]` below, matching the damage in the original, not a
transcription error. The poem closes with five runic characters
(ᛋ ᚱ ᛠ ᚹ ᛞ) used as a kind of signature or oath-seal rather than
spelled-out words; their exact intended reading is still debated by
scholars, so they are reproduced here as runes rather than resolved into
a specific word. Old English text: cross-checked against Kluge's
*Angelsächsisches Lesebuch* (3rd ed., 1902, Halle: Max Niemeyer), a
public-domain scholarly edition (over 120 years old). Modern English
translation: own work for this repository, prose rendering (not verse).
